Ever feel starving at 4 pm even after a proper lunch? A study that put glucose monitors on 1070 adults found that a blood-sugar DIP 2–3 hours after eating predicted hunger and later eating better than the sugar spike itself. The biggest dippers ate about 312 kcal more over the next 24 hours.

In the study, the biggest dips came after the test meal with the biggest sugar spike (a glucose drink). Fair warning: this is an association, the effect is small, food was self-reported, and 97% of participants were white — Indian meals weren't tested. Hunger has many causes; this is one.

Source: Wyatt et al., Nature Metabolism 2021 (doi:10.1038/s42255-021-00383-x)
